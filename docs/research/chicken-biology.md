# Chicken biology for Coop Chaos

Research reviewed 16 October 2026. This is a biological reference for the accepted
[settlement design](../../story/settlement-design.md) and the historical
[gameplay proposal](../../story/proposals/gameplay-rules-analysis.md).
Chicken biology supplies needs, opportunities, and constraints. Human motives,
marriage institutions, legitimacy, promises, and tribal politics belong to the
fiction defined in [the vision](../../story/vision.md).

## Growth and names

| Term | Useful meaning for the game |
| --- | --- |
| Chick | A young chicken after hatching. |
| Pullet | A young female; exhibition terminology commonly uses under one year. She may already be laying. |
| Cockerel | A young male; commonly under one year in exhibition terminology. He may already be sexually mature. |
| Hen | An adult female; ordinary usage is less strict than exhibition age classes. |
| Cock or rooster | A male chicken; rooster is often used without a strict age distinction. |
| Broody hen | A hen exhibiting incubation behavior; this is a reproductive state, not a breed or permanent occupation. |
| Clutch and brood | A clutch is a set of eggs; a brood is a group of young, commonly under maternal care. |

Pullet/cockerel age labels must not become a rule that reproduction starts on
the first birthday. Kentucky Extension distinguishes poultry categories;
Wisconsin Extension describes production pullets already beginning to lay at
18–20 weeks. Minnesota describes typical backyard onset around six months.
Breed, line, condition, and season matter. A broad **roughly 18–24 weeks** is a
useful design reference for many layers, not a deadline for every chicken.
[Kentucky poultry categories](https://afs.mgcafe.uky.edu/extension/poultry/chicken),
[Wisconsin ready-to-lay pullets](https://livestock.extension.wisc.edu/articles/judging-ready-to-lay-pullets/),
[Minnesota backyard chickens](https://extension.umn.edu/agriculture/animals-and-livestock/poultry/raising-chickens-for-eggs).

Hatchlings can move and feed early, but still benefit from maternal warmth,
protection, and learning. A review of maternal behavior describes natural care
extending across several weeks, with considerable variation. Mobility therefore
does not imply independence. [Edgar and colleagues, 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4730119/).

## Laying is different from producing a surviving chick

Hens can lay without mating; eggs produced without viable sperm are unfertilized. Egg formation takes
slightly more than a day, so daily laying is a potential rate under favorable
conditions, not a universal promise.
[Oklahoma State poultry and egg questions](https://extension.okstate.edu/fact-sheets/often-asked-questions-about-poultry-and-eggs).

Fertilization occurs before the egg receives its later coverings. Hens can
retain viable sperm for days or weeks, so a rooster's departure does not
immediately end fertility. Broodiness slows or stops laying. Chicken incubation
normally takes about **21 days**; fertility alone does not guarantee a hatch.
Delaying incubation reduces hatchability, so fertile eggs cannot form an
indefinite stockpile. [University of Maine hatching guide](https://extension.umaine.edu/publications/2072e/).

Shorter daylight, molt, and age affect production. Modern husbandry can sustain
outputs that a seasonal, unaided foraging society should not inherit by default.
[Oregon State laying changes](https://extension.oregonstate.edu/catalog/pnw-565-why-did-my-chickens-stop-laying?reference=catalog).

Design implication: distinguish **laying → fertility → incubation → hatch →
juvenile survival → maturity**. Each transition has different prerequisites.
Care must remain valuable even when it reduces immediate egg production.

## Food and habitat

Chickens are omnivores. Their diet can include seeds, plant material, insects,
and other small food items; reliable water and balanced nutrition matter.
Requirements differ with growth and reproduction. Calcium supports shell
formation; a laying diet is not automatically the right diet for a chick.
[University of Georgia nutrition](https://extension.uga.edu/publications/detail.html?number=C954&title=nutrition-for-the-backyard-flock).

Insoluble grit assists mechanical digestion in the gizzard. It is different
from a soluble calcium source such as shell material. Avoid a single magic
“mineral” that simultaneously guarantees digestion, fertility, and hatching.
[Extension avian digestion](https://poultry.extension.org/articles/poultry-anatomy/avian-digestive-system/).

Pasture contributes food but is not a dependable complete diet for productive
domestic poultry. Forage supply and nutritional value vary. An entirely
human-free game needs a deliberately designed ecology and modest reproductive
output, rather than commercial laying rates supported by invisible feed.
[Oregon State pastured poultry nutrition](https://smallfarms.oregonstate.edu/smallfarms/feeding-pastured-poultry).

Natural behaviors include foraging, nesting, perching, and dust bathing.
Space matters through what birds can do and reach, not only a total area.
Managed housing recommendations are not measurements of a wild landscape's
carrying capacity. [Merck poultry behavior](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/behavior/behavior-of-poultry/behavior-of-poultry),
[Penn State housing](https://extension.psu.edu/small-scale-poultry-housing).

## Pecking order and social recognition

Dominance relationships can settle into stable patterns in which signals and
yielding replace repeated fights. Arrivals, losses, and other disruptions can
destabilize the group. This supports remembered access relationships rather
than constantly rerolling every bird's rank.
[Merck poultry behavior](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/behavior/behavior-of-poultry/behavior-of-poultry).

In an experiment comparing groups of 10 and 120 hens, birds in small groups
responded differently to familiar and unfamiliar birds; those in large groups
did not show the same distinction in that test. Group size and conditions
change social strategies. This is **not** a universal maximum coop size.
[D'Eath and Keeling, 2003](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0168159103002168).

A study of female red junglefowl found prior social experience and aggression
more informative for dominance than age alone. “The oldest hen always rules”
would therefore be a fictional custom, not a biological law.
[Kim and Zuk, 2000](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10973726/).

Recognition and dominance do not prove human friendship or government. One
commercial laying-hen study did not find consistent close pair preferences
under its conditions. Affection, political legitimacy, promises, and grudge
ledgers in Coop Chaos are intentional personification.
[Abeyesinghe and colleagues, 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.applanim.2012.12.003).

## Roosters and maternal protection

Male chicken food calls communicate about food and vary with the social
audience. Research on red junglefowl also documents vigilance and alarm
behavior in social and mating contexts. This supports a rooster noticing danger,
calling others, or directing attention to food; it does not establish that
roosters can reliably defeat large predators.
[Evans and Evans, 1999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10458882/),
[Kazara, Oxford doctoral research, 2019](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:f476fe2b-0d27-4aa9-99c6-861e7c594081).

Multiple males can coexist while competing within a hierarchy. A small captive
red-junglefowl study found dominant males obtained more matings, while female
preferences also varied. A feral-fowl study documented both female resistance
and male coercion. Neither supports treating a male as owning a biologically
fixed number of hens, or presenting animal mating as a model of human marriage.
[Johnsen and colleagues, 2001](https://doi.org/10.1163/156853901750077754),
[Pizzari, 2001](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1088589/).

Maternal care includes more than incubation. An experiment involving broody hens
and chicks found maternal presence buffered aspects of the chicks' response to
a stressor. The result supports meaningful maternal influence, without proving
the game's proposed fostering customs or shared childcare institutions.
[Edgar and colleagues, 2015](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S000334721500144X).

## Predation and landscape tradeoffs

Poultry face aerial and ground predators, including hawks, owls, foxes, and
smaller nest threats, with the roster depending on location. Trees and shrubs
can provide overhead protection, while concealed approaches can help ground
predators. Time of activity varies; “foxes only hunt at night” is too rigid.
[Virginia Tech predator guidance, 2023](https://www.pubs.ext.vt.edu/content/dam/pubs_ext_vt_edu/APSC/apsc-192/APSC-192.pdf).

A South African study of indigenous chicks found major predator losses and
differences between vegetation settings. Its local mortality proportions should
not become universal game probabilities. Habitat, age, and exposure matter.
[Chikumba and Chimonyo, 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33241507/).

## Breed references and fictional lineages

These are useful physical and broad husbandry references, not personality
classes. Selected lines and individuals vary; an annual farm egg count does not
predict reproduction under the game's natural conditions.

| Real reference | Supported characteristics | Proposed design use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Leghorn](https://livestockconservancy.org/leghorn-chicken/) | Light, active Mediterranean breed; strong laying selection and generally low broodiness. | Mobile foraging silhouette; reproduction still constrained by nutrition and care. |
| [Rhode Island Red](https://livestockconservancy.org/heritage-breeds/heritage-breeds-list/rhode-island-red-chicken/) | Heritage and production-selected lines differ; old-type birds tend to be larger and broodier. | Show variation within a lineage; avoid one universal “Red” template. |
| [Plymouth Rock](https://livestockconservancy.org/plymouth-rock-chicken/) | Several varieties; barred plumage is distinctive; broad dual-purpose and maternal associations. | Readable barred character family with varied ambitions. |
| [Orpington](https://livestockconservancy.org/about-us/conservation-successes/orpington-chicken/) | Broad-bodied English breed developed through deliberate crosses; often described as calm. | Rounded silhouette; calmness may inform a tendency, never a moral alignment. |
| [Brahma](https://livestockconservancy.org/brahma-chicken/) | Large, feather-legged, relatively slow to mature; cold-weather associations and substantial maintenance needs. | Size and maturation tradeoffs; physical bulk does not guarantee courage or leadership. |
| [Silkie](https://breeds.okstate.edu/poultry/chickens/silkie-bantam-chickens) | Distinctive soft plumage, dark skin, extra toe, and broodiness; size classification differs by country. | Strong silhouette and potential incubation tendency; no compulsory caretaker caste. |

A breed, a plumage variety, a bantam size/type, and a commercial hybrid are
different concepts. A Barred Plymouth Rock is a variety within a breed;
“bantam” is not one breed. Modern breed histories involve human selection and
place names. Use them as author-facing references; fictional lineage names can
fit the world without introducing farmers into its story.
[Livestock Conservancy heritage FAQ](https://livestockconservancy.org/heritage-chicken-definition/heritage-chicken-faq/).

Domestic chicken ancestry is primarily associated with red junglefowl, with
later genetic contributions complicating a single pure-ancestor story.
[Wang and colleagues, 2020](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41422-020-0349-y).
The game need not explain domestication or claim to reconstruct a prehistoric
wild flock. Mixed lineages should inhabit the same coop; lineage is not culture,
caste, nationality, or virtue.

## How to use the evidence

The studies concern different populations: commercial hens, captive junglefowl,
feral birds, and village chickens. Their findings establish useful constraints
and possibilities, not one universal chicken society. Extension husbandry
guidance often assumes feed, housing, and human care. The proposed game removes
those supports and deliberately adds human social motives. Keep these changes
explicit, and tune the resulting fiction through play rather than presenting
balance constants as measured biology.
