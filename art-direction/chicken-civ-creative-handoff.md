# Chicken Civ: creative design handoff

24 October 2026 · Written-design handoff with exploratory gameplay concepts

This brief now accompanies exploratory gameplay concepts generated at the user's
request after written design. It is not completed visual design or authority to
produce runtime assets. Warfare and territorial conquest are the main goal.
The [gameplay draft](../story/proposals/chicken-civ-design.md) and
[narrative draft](../story/proposals/chicken-civ-narrative.md) are the source packet;
their particular mechanics, names and numbers remain proposals.

The [Long Crow progression specification](../story/proposals/chicken-civ-progression.md)
adds a tech dependency diagram, twelve techniques, careers, offices and succession.
Its Knotyard, fitted supply pack, call practice, Empty Perch and one siege engine
are concrete next visual subjects. Show expertise through practiced action and
equipment, authority through a restrained sign of office, and remembrance through
a meaningful place. Avoid making character level synonymous with larger bodies
or hereditary costume. These are written concepts, not approved visual designs.

The existing [visual bible](visual-bible.md) and [asset pipeline](asset-pipeline.md)
retain the original inhabited chicken landscape, editable-asset requirements
and actual-camera acceptance standard. The generated concepts below are
exploratory and have not been accepted by the user. No production model or
runtime gameplay has been produced for this handoff. The current scene's visual
acceptance remains unproven.

## Creative questions to resolve next

1. **A chicken army at readable scale.** Give Guard, Raider and Slinger distinct
   silhouettes, posture, movement and equipment while preserving individual
   identity. Equipment must be operable by beaks and feet. Neither sex nor tree
   family defines a role. Resolve the Slinger's loading and firing action before
   promising its final visual design.
2. **A contested landscape.** Show home groves, productive commons, approaches,
   local stores and occupation positions clearly. Control, contest, isolation
   and abandonment need different readable states. A map tint cannot replace
   the garrison and actual work that cause control.
3. **Tree people under military pressure.** Keep tree anatomy and personality
   visible through installed works. A fortification should look like a chicken
   community adapting its home, not a human castle replacing the tree. Military
   possession and the tree's affiliation must not share one ambiguous symbol.
4. **Troops and supplies.** Make carried food, stone ammunition, kits, casualties
   and delivered stock recognizable without turning every unit into a floating
   icon. Worn equipment and fatigue should remain legible in the elevated view.
5. **Communities with character.** Explore Rootward and Highcanopy through customs,
   materials, insignia and assembly habits. They share chicken personhood and
   may contain mixed tree families. Avoid biological faction castes and universal
   good/evil visual coding. All proposed names remain open for creative revision.
6. **Command without losing the world.** Develop squad selection, clear orders,
   a rally marker, supply warning, capture progress and an optional map overlay.
   Inspect the first small battle at the actual fixed camera. Larger maps need
   an explicit later navigation decision; no pan/zoom behavior changes here.
7. **Combat and aftermath.** Show commitment, impact, withdrawal, evacuation and
   return to work. Distinguish an injured bird from a dead one. Preserve reduced
   motion and readable state without relying on screen shake or flashing.

## Suggested artifact sequence

Start with a coherent direction sheet containing a small battle in its landscape,
three role silhouettes, one work-and-war character, and a contested grove. Then
explore the characters and communities, structures and resources, interface,
and motion. Assess selected alternatives against the written rules before
expanding into a full asset kit.

For each design, identify which gameplay fact it communicates and how it reads
at strategy scale. The first composition should show an actual tactical question:
a guarded approach, a possible flank, a supply journey and a place to retreat.
Do not make a cinematic battle that cannot be understood or commanded in play.

Concept approval later must be distinct from mesh/rig quality and runtime proof.
Production assets require editable sources, provenance, export contracts and
integrated inspection. No concept sheet, rendering or handoff alone demonstrates
a working combat system or production-ready art.
