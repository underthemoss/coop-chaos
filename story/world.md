# World and social rules

This file owns established world constraints. The playable example in
[story-system.md](story-system.md) is provisional.

- **A coop is a tribe.** It has relationships, customs, rivalries, and a shared
  future. Ground life and natural branch roosting precede construction; settled
  homes grow into treehouses and canopy neighborhoods in forest clusters, with
  supporting ground work and productive open commons between them. Chickens
  deliberately cultivate, weave, store, and build structures
  with beaks and feet. These are fictional capabilities; humans and farmers
  do not exist in this world.
- **A tree is someone as well as somewhere.** Named individual trees have
  personalities and histories; the expression of their consciousness remains a
  design proposal. Species-rooted families are cultural kinship groups, not formal botanical
  ranks; they are separate from individual ancestry and silhouette/growth form. Chickens may affiliate naturally but can
  cross freely and change ties. A chicken's attachment and a tree's relationship
  with its community are distinct, evolving facts. [Tree kinships](tree-kinships.md)
  owns this direction; exact family names, characters and rules remain proposals.
- **A hen is a character.** Her position in the pecking order affects what she
  can claim, but does not replace her preferences, history, or relationships.
- **A rooster's protection is social.** He may earn allegiance by defending his
  flock, owe obligations within it, and face rival roosters outside it. Pacts
  change those obligations; hostility is not an immutable alignment flag.
- **Eggs are future lives.** Hatching connects generations and kinship. Eggs
  are not a commercial output or sale loop.
- **Kinship crosses borders.** Marriage and household moves can secure a pact,
  divide loyalties, or carry a grievance into another coop. Preserve individual
  history and the response of everyone affected; transfers are not inventory moves.
- **Memory matters.** Public slights, care, promises, and betrayal should change
  future behavior or options. Gossip and testimony are possible future mechanisms,
  not implemented systems.

The player guides the whole coop while individual chickens retain their own
motives and can accept, negotiate, resist, or abandon an assignment. The accepted
[settlement design](settlement-design.md) owns construction, production,
transport, expansion, and the emergence of politics through logistics. The thin construction-delivery and roost proof now implements a small subset;
production, expansion, threats and politics remain future. The
[earlier gameplay analysis](proposals/gameplay-rules-analysis.md) is background,
not a competing construction or progression plan.

Unsettled: the player's in-world identity; exact recipes, rates, starting cast and
counts, calendar, predator species, and behavioral algorithms; detailed coop
institutions and limits of major conflict. Prototype explicitly without silently
promoting a balance assumption or historical example into canon.

## Forest homes and invented access

Forest clusters are the homes from which kingdoms grow. Treehouses, trunk access,
and canopy neighborhoods are the construction focus. Open commons between them
are productive shared no-man's-land, with bounded mixed-food foraging and future richer ecology; they are
neither empty nor owned automatically by the nearest tree. Repeated shared field
use can later produce pacts and rivalry. Map geography alone does not simulate
territory or safety. Mixed botanical families can support complementary spaces,
materials and knowledge through actual work and exchange. Single-family kingdoms
can thrive and trade; no species is a mandatory collectible or automatic modifier.

The opening four adults begin on the ground and can fly to natural branches at
night. Future chicken lineages are fictional breed-inspired varieties within
chickens, not separate species, professions or moral castes. Their bodies must
have credible access: do not generalize the fixture's flight to all later birds.
Low-flight bodies need usable low perches or access suited to them; construction
improves a viable starting life rather than making sleep possible for the first
time. Improved woven treehouses provide sheltered berths and deliberate ladder access. Chickens
can invent machinery: hauling rigs, mechanical lifts, and eventually science-fiction
elevators and defenses belong to accepted progression direction. Their exact
recipes and capabilities remain proposals in [technology design](technology.md).
No human builders or assets are implied. Defensive effects must answer particular
threats; tree height never supplies generic invulnerability.
