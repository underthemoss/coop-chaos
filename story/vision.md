# A small world. Long memories.

## Established premise

Coop Chaos is a chicken settlement builder that personifies human social and
political drama through the individuals running its economy.
They have daily lives, pride, affection, fear, ambition, grudges, and loyalty.
Comedy comes from taking their dignity seriously. Emotional credibility keeps
choices from becoming an optimization spreadsheet.

There are no humans or farmers. Coops are tribes and communities, not necessarily
human-built sheds. Hens have lives and a pecking order. Roosters can be chivalrous
and protective inside their coop, and antagonistic toward rivals unless pacts
change that relationship. Eggs hatch into the next generation; they are not sold.

Kinship and marriage moves between coops can secure alliances. Named hens retain
preferences, relationships, loyalties, and consequences through those moves.
Arrangements may be welcomed, resisted, coerced, or betrayed. Their agency must
be represented even when they cannot control every event. No character is a
fungible resource.

Human patterns provide fictional inspiration across many cultures and
individual experiences. They are not claims that all people, hens, or roosters
behave in one way. Literal zoology does not outrank the social story.

## Experience to protect

The player guides the whole coop while individual chickens retain their own
motives. Community priorities and decisions influence their lives; they do not
erase personal wants, resistance, or relationships.

The [accepted settlement design](settlement-design.md) owns construction,
production, player-built paths and junctions, local storage, autonomous deliveries,
and expansion. Chickens deliberately cultivate, weave, store, and build with
beaks and feet. Biology supplies constraints; civilization is the fiction.

The player should enjoy building and diagnosing a working settlement. Individual
motives become visible through work, repeated encounters, and reactions. Access
to food, routes, and protection can create social power; districts later develop
loyalties and pacts. Consequential systems connect economic/spatial decisions to
observable work, individual consequences, and persistent responses. Story does
not require mandatory conversation or a moral dilemma for every construction.

The immediate target is an art-led living sandbox: a beautiful inhabited grove,
basic foraging and building, and a few direct actions with autonomous responses.
No authored arc or mandatory first-home sequence organizes play. The
[opening brief](../art-direction/opening-view-exploration.md) owns the actual asset
and fixed-camera proof; forage inputs, depletion, regrowth and preferences remain
proposals rather than implemented or accepted tuning.

A later substantial target is an enjoyable roughly thirty-minute settlement
loop and recognition of a bird by its behavior. Its loss should affect the
economy and the player emotionally. These outcomes require playtesting and are
not proven by the current demo.

Stats support visible drama. Do not add a loyalty meter without a relationship
and behavior that explains it. Prefer one legible consequence to a page of
anonymous modifiers. Avoid deterministic moral labels that declare one choice
universally good.

## Tree kingdoms and open commons

Construction centers on treehouses and access. Forest clusters grow into kingdoms:
named individual trees, shared landings and canopy homes become social places.
Trees are central characters with personalities and remembered histories, not
interchangeable building sockets. Their botanical species root cultural families, distinct
from personal ancestry, growth form and formal taxonomic ranks. Chickens can feel a natural affinity for
a tree and still cross freely, change allegiance, or make another home. A tree's
relationship with its community also develops through their shared history.
[Tree kinships](tree-kinships.md) owns these accepted principles and the proposed
ways to express them. Mixed-family kingdoms gain possibilities through complementary
places, materials, knowledge and real work; a single-family community remains viable.
No species count grants an automatic bonus or a compulsory collection gate.

Open fields are productive no-man's-land between those homes, with future forage,
insects and contested shared journeys. Neither woodland nor tree height guarantees
safety. Ground work supplies trunk infrastructure and canopy life.

The opening flock begins on the ground and its four adult fixtures can fly to
natural branches at evening before any construction. This does not establish
flight for every future body: low-flight or flightless birds need suitable natural
low perches and accessible routes, with ladders and later inventions expanding
what they can use. Woven treehouses and ladders earn better sheltered
capacity. Chicken-invented hauling rigs, mechanical lifts, and late science-fiction
elevators develop that direction; see [Life above the ground](technology.md).
They remain ingenious chicken civilization, not miniature human suburbs.

## Present scope

Browser, single player first, multiplayer later. True 3D modeled animated birds
at an elevated strategy camera. The user's
[Kingdoms and Castles reference](https://store.steampowered.com/app/569480/Kingdoms_and_Castles/)
means a broad inhabited low-poly landscape, not a character pedestal. The current
Node-authored GLBs are visually rejected against opening-view-v2 and remain
functional placeholders. High-quality authored world, tree, chicken, resource and
building assets, plus polished movement/work poses, still need an actual authoring
route and live browser proof. The concept images do not supply those artifacts.

The current thin proof has four provisional adults hauling finite reeds through
player-built paths to a useful treehouse or supporting local store. Natural flight,
ladder access, saved rest, and a contributor's attachment connect work to home.
Tree characterhood, botanical families and reciprocal community relationships
remain design direction; the saved contributor attachment is a small existing
behavior, not those full systems. The larger settlement loop, food, reproduction,
threats, kingdoms and inventions are unimplemented. The old first-evening cast
and dilemma remain historical fixtures, without privileged status or a required sequel.
