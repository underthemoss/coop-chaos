# Chicken Civ buildings and the life inside them

27 October 2026 · Building story and design proposal

**Status:** a proposal for review before implementation. Warfare and territorial conquest are the accepted goal; the architecture below develops the existing [gameplay](chicken-civ-design.md), [Long Crow progression](chicken-civ-progression.md), and [narrative](chicken-civ-narrative.md) proposals. New names, layouts, building behavior and story scenes remain provisional. This does not add buildings, combat, capacity or technology to a saved game. The [building art direction](../../art-direction/chicken-civ-buildings.md) contains the concept boards and production handoff.

## The architectural idea

A chicken kingdom should look like **a living tree learning to hold a civilization**. Homes occupy the forks. Goods collect at the roots. Ladders, landings and hauling baskets make the trunk a busy connection. Watchers face the approaches; workshops face the common ground. The tree remains the largest, oldest presence in the composition.

Each building buys a useful possibility and creates a commitment. A store shortens a supply journey but must be stocked. A second workshop permits parallel work only if another adult and more material are available. A defended grove extends the frontier while demanding meals and relief. This preserves the causal structure behind the StarCraft study: production, expansion, technology and immediate fighting strength compete for the same scarce effort. Chicken Civ gives that effort names, homes and memories.

Architecture must communicate those choices before the player reads a panel. A half-woven pack, an empty receiving shelf, a silent watch platform and an unfinished counterweight tell different stories. Decorative activity must never pretend an idle building is producing.

## A morning before the Long Crow

*Illustrative scene using the proposed campaign cast, not a mandatory opening or a scripted outcome.*

The first shelter at Reedwake was still called the New House, although its rainhood had been patched three times. Bracken had promised to finish the second alcove before the next expedition. This morning a strip of reed roofing lay where he had left it. Beneath it, his sleeping mat had acquired a very small puddle.

At the foot of the tree, Tansy counted the departure baskets and moved one back under the depot awning.

“That one is for coming home.”

Bracken looked toward the Muster Court, where Sedge had arranged the practice screens to reproduce the narrow crossing. The gap was much less dignified than the one in his speech. Two birds could pass it comfortably, provided neither insisted on being first.

The Knotyard was already occupied. A carrier needed a replacement pack. The same jig could hold the trial assembly for the expedition's new supply harness, but not while the old pack was being repaired. A second jig would help; someone would still have to work it. Tansy knew exactly who would be asked.

Moss had placed a folded litter beside Wingrest's entrance. She wanted the expedition to succeed. She also wanted Bracken's promise of a relief shift to mean a particular morning, with a particular bird standing beside the beds. She had begun taking her own meals at the shelter because otherwise nobody noticed she was missing them.

On the lower branch, the household porch faced the loading court. It had been built there so returning workers could find company without climbing through the sleeping places. Now it meant a commander could not leave without passing the people who had packed his supper.

Sedge came back from the Watch Perch with a useful observation: the opposing scouts had checked the ford twice, but nobody had appeared on the long eastern approach. It was a report about what she had seen, not a promise that the route was empty. Bracken asked her to show the squad. He would have preferred to announce that he had already thought of it.

They left the nursery's supply baskets where they were. They left one adult on relief duty. They carried less stone than Bracken had first wanted, because a carrier carrying stone could not also carry food.

By evening the second alcove might still be unfinished. If the expedition returned with its people and unused provisions, Tansy would remember who had kept the reserve. If the relief bird failed to arrive, Moss would remember that too. Reedwake would remain a home even if somebody else's occupation crest stood beside its roots.

The scene's playable causes are ordinary: one occupied workshop slot, real carried loads, a care assignment, observed terrain and a departure decision. The memories follow what actually happens. No building forces these personalities or this ending.

## One roster with distinct jobs

These are ten functional roles, not ten mandatory opening buildings. Descriptive names remain visible alongside proposed local names so the player can recognize what each place does.

| Place | Role and silhouette | Strategic commitment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Boughhome** — sheltered home | Two folded reed hoods around an open branch porch | Reachable household space and construction effort |
| **Root Nursery** — dependent care | Low crescent shelter and broad ground threshold | Care time, local typed food, and future household capacity |
| **Second Supper** — home supply store | Three plaited containers and a receiving porch | Stock held at one useful but vulnerable location |
| **Knotyard** — assembly and trials | Crooked forked jig, fan of panels, open work floor | One production or research slot and its worker |
| **Muster Court** — training and fitting | Open horseshoe court with practice forks | An existing adult, an existing kit and training time |
| **Watch Perch** — observation | Small outward-facing platform and distinctive forked call frame | A present watcher and an actual view |
| **Field Store** — forward supply | Low paired panniers beneath a short removable hood | Deliveries that sustain distance from home |
| **Holding Perch** — occupation post | Small raised duty platform, local crest and relief rail | Garrison, uncontested presence and continuing provisions |
| **Wingrest** — recovery shelter | Two shallow wing-shaped hoods opening onto a quiet court | Caregivers, food, reachable beds and time |
| **Boughwright Bay** — siege apparatus | Broad low assembly frame and heavy counterweight cradle | Late engineering, materials, operators and escort |

The Empty Perch is a civic place within a shared porch or court, not another research building. Ladders, loading landings, hauling baskets, screens and bridges are infrastructure with their own purpose, not decorative buildings that secretly grant bonuses.

The roster groups occupied places; it does not remove the gameplay proposal's **thornwork and woven screens**. Those remain separately placed defensive improvements. Thornwork delays a particular ground approach under its eventual movement rules. A Screened Approach section obstructs ranged sight through its occupied space, including friendly shots, and can be flanked or damaged under the proposed military-work rules. Neither supplies universal cover, control or tree safety. Their low woven silhouettes belong beside the frontier kit, with openings that show the usable route.

## Boughhome

**Life.** A home is a cluster of sleeping places and a shared porch fitted around one useful fork. Its first roof need not cover the entire deck. Two curved rainhoods leave the living trunk exposed, while a visible gap gives the household an open place to greet returning neighbors. A repaired hood and a moved sleeping mat can eventually record a household's history; those visual histories require saved facts when implemented.

**Play.** It supplies only the reachable sheltered places that its actual layout supports. A bigger silhouette does not create population capacity. Natural roosts remain useful. Construction has visible delivered material, floor, shelter and access stages; occupancy waits for the required stages. A ladder landing needs room for the bird and its carried load before anyone climbs past it.

**Tension.** Bracken's unfinished alcove gives his military ambition a domestic cost. Tansy wants a home she can reach after a late delivery. Neither story assigns domestic work by sex. Another household might prefer an exposed porch with a good view to an inner shelter, provided the actual conditions support that choice.

**Growth.** Add a supported side alcove, a second approach or a connecting porch where the tree can carry it. Preserve the old construction within the new. No compulsory upgrade turns every home into the same tower.

## Root Nursery

**Life.** A quiet ground shelter nestles between roots, with a warm inner nest and a sheltered outside court. Its broad threshold is nearly flush with the ground. The roof shelters without hiding the caregiver, dependent young or delivered meals. The host tree is not carved into a cave.

**Play.** Preserve the current meaning: the implemented nursery has two dependent care places, no adult berths, local staple and protein supplies, and a completed-home prerequisite. The new art does not change those counts. Eggs are future named people. A nursery is never a barracks, army purchase menu or instant source of adult replacements. Short battles must work with starting adults.

**Tension.** Mobilization can take a prospective relief carer away. Show the uncovered duty before departure. A willing rooster may deliver food or take an appropriate care shift; laying and active brood rules remain attached to the actual named hen and current simulation. Do not infer family relationships from two birds standing together.

**Growth.** Improve shelter, food handling and accessible care space before considering upper broodrooms. Elevated care waits for real routes usable by grounded young and their carriers; a pretty hanging basket is not that route.

## Second Supper

**Life.** “The first supper is the one you packed. The second is the one somebody remembered you would need.” The proposed depot name shares the quartermaster's existing title, Keeper of the Second Supper. It is a place of arrivals, sorting and promises made against actual stock.

**Play.** This is the home form of the existing proposed local-store system. Staples, protein, reeds and stone remain separate inventory categories. Grain can read through an open seed basket; protein belongs in a distinct covered container; stone sits low, away from food and floor edges. Those container shapes communicate type, not new food-processing mechanics. Incoming loads arrive at the front; outgoing carriers use a clear side lane. An upper household may have a small receiving shelf, but heavy stock stays near the ground unless a real hauling route justifies lifting it.

**Tension.** Tansy can support a campaign while rejecting an unsupported dispatch target. Her presence enables only the duties assigned to her office, never a passive percentage bonus. Existing delivery jobs continue if she leaves; promises, priorities and the uncovered coordinating duty remain legible.

**Growth.** Add covered bays or a second local depot where travel warrants it. Reserve food is physically here, not a glowing global supply bar. A rival can threaten the route without the building magically withdrawing supplies from distant soldiers.

## Knotyard

**Life.** The settlement's inventive pride is a crooked forked frame, tension cords, foot-bracing stops and enough clear ground to argue around a prototype. Jigs do the gripping that hands would otherwise do. A beak draws a strand; a foot pins it; another bird steadies the frame. Its useful mess is concentrated in the current job.

**Play.** Retain the Long Crow's assembly-and-experiment owner. One work slot makes equipment or performs one trial; the same slot cannot do both. A second Knotyard adds capacity only with another worker and delivered inputs. Finished kits must reach named adults. Research does not equip the whole army, and the finished trial object is not duplicated when knowledge becomes shared.

**Tension.** A new pack trial displaces shield production or a repair. A skilled hen may prefer teaching to office; a rooster may gain standing by solving a recurring cargo problem. Neither becomes a master because the building upgraded. Practice and useful service belong to the individual.

**Growth.** Add apparatus for new techniques to the same recognizably old yard. The shared tech ladder persists through building loss; restoring apparatus restores opportunities to work and teach. Research intent is visible through the object and materials when a scout can actually observe them.

## Muster Court

**Life.** A low horseshoe of woven screens encloses an open practice ground. Two crooked forks hold replaceable targets; a fitted-kit rack sits beside the entrance. The captain's speaking perch is barely higher than everyone else's, although this distinction may be much discussed.

**Play.** It trains or changes equipment for one existing adult at a time in the initial proposal. The Knotyard supplies kits; the court supplies practice. A training adult is unavailable for other work during that time. Call and Answer is demonstrated with actual participants, then taught; a second court without another available adult, kit and food is only empty capacity. Specific drills reserve their actual participants. No birds emerge from the building.

**Tension.** Sedge can demonstrate the approach she found, while Bracken chooses whether to credit her. A rooster returning from injury may teach a drill while a hen leads it in the field. Expertise, appointment and social deference remain distinct.

**Growth.** Change targets and practice geometry for Brace and Turn or Measured Volley. The initial research booking remains at the Knotyard; rehearsals use the court's available space and participating birds. Both occupations must be respected when a trial spans the two places. No duplicate research button creates a second discovery.

## Watch Perch

**Life.** A narrow outward-facing balcony looks along an approach. Its forked call frame and small hanging reed vanes give it a clear silhouette without a human bell tower. The frame is a visual identity; the proposed observation and alarm rules decide what a watcher can report.

**Play.** Elevation helps only through actual sight and terrain. Canopy can obstruct the watcher; the platform does not reveal everything in a circle or see through fog. Unstaffed means no active watcher. The Survey Perch technique adds practiced surveying to an ordinary supported watch position, rather than requiring an already unlocked Survey Perch to research itself.

**Tension.** Flint's useful report may reveal a vulnerable supply landing in a grove he still considers home. His attachment does not make him an ally or omniscient informer. Sedge wants the report credited accurately, including its uncertainty and age.

**Growth.** Improve a useful observation angle, access and relief arrangement. Raising the tower ever higher is not the universal upgrade. Leave the tree's most distinctive fork visible.

## Field Store and Holding Perch

**Life.** At the frontier the architecture becomes lower, leaner and easier to inspect. A Field Store has two covered panniers, an unloading mat and little else. Nearby, a Holding Perch is a small duty platform with a recognizable coop crest and space for the incoming relief to meet the outgoing guards.

**Play.** These are separate functions. The Field Store is another location in the same typed-stock and reservation system as Second Supper. It buys shorter final supply trips, not free food or a replenishment aura. The Holding Perch is the occupation post from the gameplay proposal: only eligible objectives, actual presence, contest and sustained provision establish control. It can reference a nearby field store's delivered stock; it never creates a second copy of that inventory. A crest alone establishes nothing.

**Tension.** Vetch may regain the post while households remain attached to Reedwake and distrust her. Bracken may hold it while failing to relieve the very birds who won it. The tree's affiliation, household ties and military possession require distinct presentation.

**Growth.** A remote camp may eventually acquire a second receiving place and more shelter. Its strategic liability remains the journey back to dependable supply. There is no requirement to build a road. A ground trail in art indicates traffic, not player-built connectivity or a speed bonus.

## Wingrest

**Life.** Two shallow rainhoods curve like resting wings over low recovery bays. A quiet forecourt provides room for a two-bird litter to turn without lifting a patient into the canopy. One side is open to companions; the other stores care materials and meals. This is recovery space for adults, visually distinct from the Root Nursery's small enclosed nest.

**Play.** A shelter alone does not heal. Care needs a present caregiver, reachable patient, food and time under the proposed injury rules. Splint and Shelter and Two-Bird Litter remain techniques in the existing Care branch. A litter occupies two carriers; it does not teleport its passenger or stabilize them merely by moving.

**Tension.** Moss wants relief and influence over commitments whose costs arrive here. Bracken surviving an injury can change how he understands that duty. Care must not reduce Moss to a moral speech or Bracken to a required heroic death. Their next actions depend on what happened and who kept a promise.

**Growth.** Additional bays require caregivers and supply; they do not multiply a healing aura. Quiet access should be separate from an ammunition loading lane. Refuge claims during occupation need explicit later rules; architecture alone does not make people invulnerable.

## Boughwright Bay

**Life.** The impressive invention is mostly a large problem laid sideways. Two low forked supports hold a counterweight cradle over a broad assembly floor. Pebbles in a basket become an argument about balance; a release cord has a conspicuously good perch beside it. Its silhouette is horizontal and heavy, unlike the slender Watch Perch.

**Play.** This is the siege-workshop role, presented first as an apparatus bay attached to a Knotyard. For the initial design it shares that yard's booked work slot. A later independent production slot would be an explicit paid/staffed expansion, not a free extra queue hidden in an art module. Heavy stock and completed equipment stay on the ground.

Boughwright Bay is the proposed name for that occupied apparatus worksite, **not an additional prerequisite building or technology**. The initial Gatebreaker trial uses the existing Knotyard and its listed prerequisites; its existing trial recipe includes the prototype and practice apparatus. The bay illustration must not silently add another building bill or create a second free prototype. A permanent independent siege workshop would require a later explicit design.

The first concept subject is the **Gatebreaker**, following Counterweight Haul and Screened Approach in Broken Gate. It still needs two operators, a vulnerable approach and the existing proposed installation/attack rules. It attacks eligible installed military works, never chickens or living trees. Destroying a post does not occupy a grove. The Stonebow remains in the tech design but is not a second machine to solve in this art pass.

**Tension.** A coop can see the engine taking shape while its raiding force stays small. Its builders may be proud; carriers may know exactly which reserve paid for it. The machine's success cannot erase that commitment.

**Growth.** Upgrade apparatus, handling space and protection as the engineering branch grows. The concept painting demonstrates visual identity, not a mechanically validated release, force path or crew animation.

## The Empty Perch and everyday civic space

Leave one modest perch on a shared porch, with room for a gathering below. The existing Empty Perch custom concerns absence, loss and succession; an absent office holder's place remains empty at the first gathering after a loss while necessary work continues through the deputy. It is not a permanent memorial throne, a new research currency or an automatic royalty tier.

The same porch can host meals, teaching and council discussion at different times. Civic importance emerges from use. Do not build a compulsory palace to make the settlement look advanced. A repaired rail, an honored promise and the bird who now takes responsibility can carry more meaning than another roof.

## How buildings follow the technology ladder

| Existing horizon | Architecture becomes able to support | What the player can observe |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Before and during **First Muster** | Home stores, Knotyard, Muster Court, basic watch and recovery places; homes and nursery sustain settlement life | Delivered kit ingredients, one trial in progress, named adults practicing and workers displaced |
| **Long Reach** | Forward stores, relief organization, fitted packs, counterweight handling, screens and litter operations | A longer actual supply journey, occupied loading positions, real screened approaches and returning casualties |
| **Broken Gate** | Heavy apparatus at Boughwright Bay and the proposed siege equipment | Stock concentrated in engineering, operators committed, escort and ammunition needs where applicable |
| **Sunlit Canopy**, later | Powered access and dense service infrastructure | A separate future energy and maintenance model, not glowing trim on current buildings |

No row is an automatic town-wide age upgrade. A building's construction, a technique's demonstrated knowledge, an individual's learned practice and an appointment are different facts. A developed home can exist beside a modest military force; a successful early army can leave its engineering bay unbuilt.

Preserve all twelve existing techniques: Packed Provision, Call and Answer, Splint and Shelter, Survey Perch, Relief Chain, Counterweight Haul, Brace and Turn, Measured Volley, Screened Approach, Two-Bird Litter, Gatebreaker and Stonebow. This building pass adds no thirteenth unlock. The first rules experiment remains Packed Provision, Call and Answer and Splint and Shelter, after the base conquest loop is proved.

## Materials and construction that mean something

The visual palette uses living supports, weathered timber, woven reeds, cord lashings, moss and a small amount of stone. The economic proposal currently accounts for reeds, staple food, protein and stone. **The paintings' timber members do not settle a timber recipe.** Living host trees are never generic harvest nodes. A later implementation must either justify those members with an explicit fallen-material supply rule or reduce fabricated wood in the initial kit to match existing recipes. Do not charge reeds and silently manufacture an unlimited timber economy. This material decision stays open before modeling a build sequence.

Use an honest sequence: mark a usable site; deliver the actual inputs; assemble supported structure; fit floor and shelter; finish required access; commission the useful places. A construction pile must shrink as it becomes installed material. Interrupted work stays where it actually stopped. The visual state should distinguish reserved, delivered, installed, working, unstaffed, damaged and abandoned wherever the chosen rules support those states.

Keep weathering purposeful: moss near damp shaded roots, rubbed edges at regular foot contact, repairs where a part was replaced. Random broken rails make a finished building look unusable. A darkened board in concept art does not imply decay or maintenance rules have been added.

## Scale and settlement composition

Design against a consistent adult, not a constantly resized chicken. The current valley/tree study uses adult render scale `.45625`; exported adult height is approximately 0.68–0.82 m at that presentation scale. Use a 0.75 m standing adult as an art ruler, then qualify with the actual tallest/widest body, tail and cargo.

For concept planning, try 1.0–1.2 m clear single-file passages and 1.5–1.8 m at unloading/turning places. These are visual allowances, not collision or accessibility certification. A nominal 4 m platform can lose much of its floor to the trunk, rails and access opening. Occupancy requires measured usable area, supported loads and reachable places; three painted terraces do not establish a population cap.

Keep heavy production and stores on the ground, homes on lower supported forks, and watch positions where the view is useful. Reserve an open ground court and a clear canopy window facing the strategy camera. Prefer one strong structural mass, one distinctive roof or apparatus silhouette, and a few useful details per building. Filling every branch with a building would destroy both the tree's character and the player's ability to read work.

## What to choose before implementation

First review the Boughhome, Second Supper, Knotyard and Root Nursery as a coherent home kit. Their supports, openings, materials and relative scale should agree. The frontier kit then tests whether military commitments are readable without turning the grove into a human castle.

The first later implementation should prove one Boughhome and its usable access on the existing inhabited tree, with actual birds and cargo at the tactical camera. Resolve timber accounting and measured room capacity before translating the concept into construction rules. Then prove one store-to-Knotyard-to-adult equipment journey. Military systems require their own authorized implementation phase; a painted Muster Court is not that system.

Open decisions are material accounting, measured footprints and access, civilian building damage/occupation treatment, and tested combat/economic tuning. They do not prevent reviewing story and visual direction now. No final prices, production times, damage values or capacity increases are approved by this proposal.
